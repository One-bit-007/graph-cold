=== TITLE ===
When Does Graph Structure Help Noisy-Label Intrusion Detection? A Leakage-Audited Study of Rare-Class Training-Signal Retention

=== ABSTRACT (approx. 240 words, matches manuscript) ===
Machine-learning intrusion detection is trained on imperfect labels, and standard noisy-label pipelines delete suspicious samples---often the rare-class rows that carry most of the learnable signal. We report a leakage-audited boundary study of when graph structure genuinely helps noisy-label network intrusion detection. Starting from a graph extension of collaborative label denoising (CoLD), we froze an audited protocol---label-construction proxy fields masked, strongest-available leakage-controlled splits (strict entity-disjoint evaluation is feasible only for CESNET; the CICIDS and UNSW limitations are measured and characterized), pre-registered hypothesis families with Holm correction---and re-evaluated everything inside it on CICIDS-2017, UNSW-NB15, and the CESNET-TLS-Year22 service-classification stress test. Three findings survive. First (robust; 9/9 corrected tests): within a shared pipeline, soft retention improves tail-class F1 over hard deletion (+0.21 to +0.31 on CICIDS-2017; +0.025 to +0.052 elsewhere) at no detectable aggregate cost, and the direction survives real labelling errors and a 100k-window scale check. Second (null; reported with effect ceilings): a multi-view label-space graph diagnostic adds no detectable increment over Confident Learning (18/18 tests; Tail-F1 ceiling +0.026), and the specific rescue weighting adds nothing over a simple frequency floor. Third, a four-factor leakage decomposition attributes 25--36 points of Tail-F1 inflation to duplicates, cross-split entities, proxy fields, and clean-label oracle paths---factors that flatter deletion-style baselines and compress the apparent advantage of retention. The audit also yields dataset-level findings: CESNET's released features contain direct label parents; UNSW-NB15's official partition ships with 38.4% exact train--test feature collisions; and ~95% of CICIDS-2017's rare "Web Attack -- Brute Force" class are labelling artefacts. The protocol, regression tests, and reproduction package accompany the paper.
