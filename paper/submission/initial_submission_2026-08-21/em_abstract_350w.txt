Intrusion-detection classifiers are increasingly trained on labels that are delayed, rule-generated, or inconsistent across telemetry sources. A common remedy is dataset purification: detect suspicious labels and delete them before training. In security operations this is risky, because deleted instances are frequently the rare, boundary attack samples analysts most need. We argue that in security machine learning, suspicious does not mean useless, and recast label denoising as evidence-preserving data curation rather than purification. We revisit CoLD, a collaborative label-denoising framework, and ask whether lifting it to a multi-view graph and replacing hard deletion with evidence-preserving soft weighting improves noisy-label intrusion detection, through reproducible experiments on CICIDS-2017, CESNET-TLS-Year22, and UNSW-NB15. First, a label-space graph consistency diagnostic (Graph-CDM) over host, IP, temporal, and where-available behavioral views yields a modest but statistically significant aggregate Macro-F1 improvement over CoLD (Holm-corrected p<0.05 on all three datasets; up to +0.11 on CICIDS-2017, no more than +0.01 on the other two), concentrated where graph views carry discriminative structure. Second, within a shared pipeline, soft weighting is statistically indistinguishable from hard deletion on aggregate Macro-F1, yet significantly improves rare/tail attack-class Macro-F1 under high asymmetric noise (Delta Macro-F1 = +0.073/+0.022/+0.040 on the three datasets; Holm p<10^-4), at no aggregate cost. Under the identical protocol, a Confident Learning anchor outperforms our weighting on all three datasets, while full Co-Teaching collapses on tail classes. Third, a leakage audit shows naive graph denoising can be inflated by duplicates (24% exact duplicates in CICIDS-2017) and clean-label signal; removing these collapses an apparent +28-point advantage to a defensible +6.7. Our bounded claim: evidence-preserving retention is a mechanism-level remedy for rare-attack evidence loss, not a universal accuracy improvement; confident-learning-precision pruning remains the performance bar for graph-based denoising.
